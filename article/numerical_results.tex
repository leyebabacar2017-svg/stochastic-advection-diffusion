\section{Numerical Results}

In this section, we illustrate the performance of the proposed finite element approximation for 
the stochastic advection--diffusion equation. All simulations were carried out using FEniCSx 
0.10.0 on a unit square domain

\[
\Omega=(0,1)^2.
\]

The diffusion coefficient was chosen as

\[
\kappa = 0.01,
\]

and the advection velocity field was taken as

\[
\mathbf b=(1,0)^T.
\]

The numerical experiments are divided into three parts. First, we validate the spatial convergence 
of the finite element discretization. Second, we verify the temporal convergence of the implicit 
Euler scheme. Finally, we investigate the stochastic model using Monte Carlo simulations.

\subsection{Spatial Convergence}

We consider the stationary advection--diffusion problem with manufactured solution

\[
\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y).
\]

The finite element approximation is computed using continuous piecewise linear elements on a sequence 
of uniform meshes.

Table~\ref{tab:spatial_convergence} reports the $L^2$ errors and the corresponding experimental orders of convergence (EOC).

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Spatial convergence of the finite element approximation.}
\label{tab:spatial_convergence}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\hline
Mesh size & $L^2$ error & EOC \\
\hline
8 & $1.428163\times10^{-2}$ & -- \\
16 & $3.616693\times10^{-3}$ & 1.98 \\
32 & $9.014219\times10^{-4}$ & 2.00 \\
64 & $2.251729\times10^{-4}$ & 2.00 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The results confirm the theoretical convergence rate

\[
O(h^2),
\]

which is expected for linear finite elements.

\subsection{Temporal Convergence}

To assess the temporal discretization error, we 
consider the transient 
advection--diffusion equation with exact solution

\[
e^{-t}
\sin(\pi x)
\sin(\pi y).
\]

The spatial mesh was fixed at \(128\times128\), while 
the time step was successively refined.

Table~\ref{tab:temporal_convergence} reports the \(L^2\) errors at the final 
time together with the corresponding experimental orders 
of convergence (EOC). As the time step is halved, the 
error is approximately reduced by a factor of two. 
The observed convergence rate is close to one for all 
refinements, which is consistent with the theoretical 
first-order accuracy of the implicit Euler method.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Temporal convergence of the implicit Euler scheme.}
\label{tab:temporal_convergence}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\hline
$\Delta t$ & $L^2$ error & EOC \\
\hline
0.1 & $6.793736\times10^{-3}$ & -- \\
0.05 & $3.452625\times10^{-3}$ & 0.98 \\
0.025 & $1.740350\times10^{-3}$ & 0.99 \\
0.0125 & $8.759994\times10^{-4}$ & 0.99 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The observed convergence rate is approximately one, in agreement with the theoretical 
first-order accuracy of the implicit Euler method:

\[
O(\Delta t).
\]

\subsection{Monte Carlo Simulation}

We now consider the stochastic advection--diffusion equation driven by additive 
Gaussian noise. The stochastic forcing is approximated through a Monte Carlo procedure, 
where independent realizations of the Wiener process are generated and the 
corresponding finite element solutions are computed.

For a fixed final time \(T=1\), the numerical approximation is obtained using 
continuous piecewise linear finite elements in space and the implicit Euler method 
in time. The noise intensity is chosen as

\[
\sigma = 0.1.
\]

The first set of results concerns the statistical properties of the numerical solution. 
Table~\ref{tab:mc_statistics} reports the mean energy, the standard deviation, and the 
variance indicators obtained from the Monte Carlo simulations.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Monte Carlo statistics at final time (T=1).}
\label{tab:mc_statistics}
\begin{tabular}{cc}
\hline
Quantity & Value \\
\hline
$N_{MC}$ & 10 \\
Mean energy & $4.679086\times10^{-2}$ \\
Standard deviation & $2.543385\times10^{-2}$ \\
Maximum variance & $1.331187\times10^{-2}$ \\
Mean variance & $4.688864\times10^{-3}$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The nonzero variance confirms the propagation of uncertainty induced by the stochastic forcing. 
At the same time, the bounded values of the statistical indicators demonstrate the stability of 
the numerical approximation.

To investigate the influence of the number of realizations, additional Monte Carlo simulations 
were performed for increasing values of \(N_{MC}\). The results are summarized in 
Table~\ref{tab:mc_convergence}.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Monte Carlo convergence study.}
\label{tab:mc_convergence}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\hline
$N_{MC}$ & Mean energy & Std energy\\
\hline
10  & $3.1373\times10^{-2}$ & $6.8768\times10^{-3}$\\
20  & $4.2183\times10^{-2}$ & $2.4151\times10^{-2}$\\
50  & $4.1863\times10^{-2}$ & $1.9006\times10^{-2}$\\
100 & $3.5494\times10^{-2}$ & $1.5533\times10^{-2}$\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The estimated mean energy remains within the same order of magnitude as the number of 
realizations increases. This behavior indicates statistical consistency of the Monte Carlo 
approximation and confirms the robustness of the stochastic finite element solver.

\subsection{Mean and Variance Fields}

The Monte Carlo simulations also provide approximations of the mean field

\[
\mathbb E[u(T)].
\]

As expected, the averaging process smooths out
part of the stochastic fluctuations and reveals
the dominant deterministic transport pattern.

The variance field

\[
\mathrm{Var}(u(T))
=
\mathbb E[u(T)^2]
-
\bigl(\mathbb E[u(T)]\bigr)^2
\]

quantifies the uncertainty induced by the stochastic forcing.
Regions with larger variance are more sensitive to random perturbations.

These quantities are visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:mean_solution}
and Figure~\ref{fig:variance_solution}.

\begin{figure}[ht]

The variance field highlights the spatial regions that are most sensitive 
to stochastic perturbations and provides quantitative information about 
uncertainty propagation in the system.

The numerical experiments confirm the theoretical results established in 
the previous sections. The finite element approximation exhibits the expected
spatial convergence rate, the implicit Euler scheme achieves first-order 
convergence in time, and the Monte Carlo simulations demonstrate stable statistical
behavior for the stochastic model.
